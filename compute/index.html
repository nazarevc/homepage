The question

Is AI compute scarce, and for how long?

Answer. Compute is scarce now. In North America it should stay scarce through 2028. The risk is delivery: most contracted capacity is not built, and the gigawatt campuses open in 2027 and 2028.

Why it is scarce now

  • US vacancy is 1.4 percent in the main leased markets. North America leased 26.5 GW in the first three quarters of 2026, and 99 percent was preleased.
  • H100 rent rose from $2.00 an hour in December 2025 to $3.10 in November 2026, on a four-year-old chip.
  • Nvidia's data center revenue was $107.6B in the latest quarter, up 110 percent.
  • JLL expects North American vacancy near zero through 2028.

Why supply lags

  • Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet and Oracle report $1.10 trillion of leases that have not started.
  • Neoclouds and their landlords run 8 to 43 percent of the power they have under contract. CoreWeave runs 1.7 GW of 4.6 GW.
  • Grid power sets the date, not money. Microsoft left Loughton because grid power was late.

What would make this wrong

  • Several gigawatt campuses that open in 2027 and 2028 have no named tenant.
  • Europe outside its five main hubs heads to 18 percent vacancy.
  • Polymarket prices H100 rent 18 percent lower by December 2027 and 33 percent lower by December 2028. Little money backs the later months.

How to read the numbers

No public register lists every data center, so this report gives market totals first and named large projects second.

  • Region. The United States and Europe in detail. Europe includes the UK, Norway, Switzerland and Iceland. Later sections cover the Middle East, Asia-Pacific, Canada, Latin America and Africa.
  • Units. Capacity is electrical power in megawatts (MW) or gigawatts (GW). 1 GW is 1,000 MW.
  • IT load. The power the servers draw. Campus power also covers cooling and losses, so it is 20 to 40 percent larger.
  • Colocation. Space a landlord leases to tenants. Self-build is space a hyperscaler (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta) owns and uses.
  • Neocloud. A cloud company that rents GPUs only, such as CoreWeave or Nebius.
  • Compute. GPU capacity for AI. Prices are US dollars per GPU-hour.
  • Source scope. CBRE counts leased capacity in main markets. Wood Mackenzie counts every disclosed project. Their totals differ by a factor of ten. The last section lists each conflict.
  • Labels. "Stated" is a company or developer claim. "Epoch est." is an Epoch AI estimate from satellite images and permits. Neither is an audit.

Lease terms

  • Take or pay. Leases now set a minimum power use of 60 to 85 percent of the allocated power.
  • Lead time. Turnkey sites prelease about 12 months before completion. Build-to-suit sites prelease about 2.5 years before.
  • Preleased capacity rose from 4,800 MW at the end of 2025 to 6,012 MW at mid-2026. Net absorption was 2,497.6 MW in 2025 and 1,456.2 MW in H1 2026.
  • Large load tariffs. New Jersey, Ohio, Oregon, Wisconsin, Florida and Delaware have approved or enacted rules similar to Virginia's tax.
  • Tenant credit. Landlords now prefer tenants with strong credit, because AI tenants weigh heavily in their portfolios.

Evidence for a shortage

  • Space in construction is 80 to 95 percent leased in the US before it opens.
  • H100 rent rose 34 to 55 percent in a year, although the chip is four years old.
  • CoreWeave's backlog grew $28.5B in one quarter.
  • JLL expects North American vacancy near zero through 2028.

Evidence against

  • Several US campuses have power and no named tenant: Sweetwater (2 GW), Project Matador (6 GW of generation permits), Project Sail in Georgia (900 MW) and Project Blue in Arizona (286 MW).
  • Buyers left sites in 2026. OpenAI dropped the Abilene extension, Stargate UK and Narvik. Microsoft left Loughton. Microsoft then took both Abilene and Narvik.
  • Money is tighter. Three of the four hyperscalers report negative free cash flow, and Microsoft cut its 2026 plan by $5B (Tech Insider, citing the New York Times and CNBC).
  • CoreWeave lost $709M in Q3 2026 and owes almost $47B (Value Add VC).
  • A third to a half of US projects due in 2026 are late. This cuts supply now, and it also means pipeline totals overstate what will open.

Timing

The large campuses carry dates of 2027 and 2028. Lighthouse, El Paso and the first PORTS-Pike phase are dated 2028, and the first EU gigafactories are due by mid-2028. The first real test for oversupply comes then.
