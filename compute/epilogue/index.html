What the bids say

  • B300 bids with a price run from $4.80 (4 nodes, 36 months, US) to $7.50 (1 node, up to 12 months, Europe). One bid offers $6.75 for 32 nodes for 6 months.
  • Most bids give no price.
  • A 36-month US bid at $4.80 sits above the cheapest long offers at $4.15 to $4.35. Price does not block long contracts. Size, start date and buyer credit are the likely blocks (my reading).
  • Epilogue's 12-month B300 offers ($6.30 to $6.60) sit below the public 12-month median of $7.18 and far below on-demand at $9.50.
  • Older chips show a wide gap. For the H100, one offer asks $3.40 and one bid pays $2.40, both for 12 months.
  • H200: one offer at $3.90 in Japan, bids from $2.85 to $3.80. B200: offers from $5.25 to $7.50, bids from $5.00 to $6.20.
  • One buyer wants 1,000 H100 chips for 12 months. One wants 800 B200 chips for 36 months in Europe.
  • Colocation bids run from 0.2 MW to 100 MW, and four ask for 20 MW or more. Two offers show a price: $110 per kW a month for 1.5 MW, and $175 per kW a month for 8.45 to 18.24 MW in Mississippi on a 10-year term.
  • European supply on the book: B300 in Spain and Iceland, B200 in Sweden at $6.55 for 12 months, and 6 MW of colocation for July 2027. European demand: three bids.

Epilogue's own statements, not verified

  • The book has carried "billions" in contract value and has 150+ suppliers.
  • Sellers offer clusters of 2 to 64 nodes on 1 to 3 year terms, plus idle H100 and H200 nodes.
  • Buyers get month-to-month to 12-month terms, one standard contract, resale rights and a 2-day test with escrow.
  • The site names Cisco and Deloitte as partners.

The home, research and partnerships pages carry no price or volume data.
